stuck: my fake SSH banner drops bots in a second

2026-07-10 · 3 min read · honeypots · stuck

Been working on HoneyJar this week (a small honeypot I want to drop on a $5 VPS to collect weird internet traffic). The idea is simple. Open some ports, log everything that hits them, ship structured events to a Discord webhook. Cheap, low touch, useful for playing with detection ideas.

The SSH listener is where I am stuck.

Almost all real traffic hitting port 22 on the open internet is bots. They connect, send their version string (something like SSH-2.0-libssh_0.10.4), wait maybe 50ms for my server to do the SSH key exchange, and then drop the connection the moment I do not respond correctly. I am logging thousands of "connected, sent 22 bytes, disconnected" events. That is not useful. What I want is the commands they try to run after they think they are in.

what I have tried

1. Just accept the TCP, read whatever comes in. Gets the version string. Nothing else. 90% of events look identical.

2. Send back a matching banner. I return SSH-2.0-OpenSSH_8.9p1 Ubuntu-3ubuntu0.1\r\n. Same result. The bot wants me to actually respond to the SSH KEX_INIT packet next, and I do not, so it hangs up.

3. Read Cowrie's source code. Cowrie is a real medium-interaction SSH honeypot. It fakes the entire SSH protocol using Twisted and Conch, and it works. But it is a lot of Twisted for something I wanted to keep small. Cowrie's requirements alone are heavier than my whole HoneyJar concept.

where I am

I think there are two honest options and I have not picked one yet.

Option A: wrap paramiko or Twisted Conch, fake just enough of the SSH KEX to trick the bot into sending its command, log it, close the connection. This gets me what I want but drags in a big dependency and a real chunk of protocol code I do not fully understand yet.

Option B: accept that HoneyJar is not supposed to be a full SSH honeypot. Let it be a "port 22 attempt logger" that tracks who is knocking, and if I want the actual command capture, I run a real Cowrie next to it. Two tiers. HoneyJar spots the noise volume, Cowrie captures the interesting bits.

Right now I am leaning toward B. HoneyJar's whole pitch was "small and easy to deploy," and turning it into a mini-Cowrie kills that. But I keep coming back to the idea that maybe I could get 80% of Cowrie in ~300 lines of asyncio and paramiko, and that would be a fun weekend project on its own.

if you have done this

If you have built a low-footprint SSH honeypot that actually gets past the version handshake without pulling in Twisted, I would love to hear how. Or if you have opinions on the two-tier approach, tell me I am wrong (or right). say hi.

Either way, I will write a follow-up when I actually ship whichever version wins.
