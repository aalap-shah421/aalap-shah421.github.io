Final season at Mason: what I'm shipping before finals

2026-05-05 · 4 min read · life · ctf · ai-security

It's the first week of May. Spring 2026 finals start next Monday. I'm typing this between a CompTIA Network+ practice block and a 6pm shift at Station 22, so this is going to be more "field notes" than "polished essay" - but here's where my head is at.

What's on my plate

  • Operating Systems final - review thread on synchronization primitives, paging, and a decent chunk of file system internals.
  • Secure RF Communications - final project on side-channel leakage from poorly shielded SDR rigs.
  • Digital Electronics - Verilog lab demo on Wednesday.
  • Systems Engineering Principles - group writeup, mostly done.
  • Two side projects (see below) and a CTF post-mortem I owe my team.

Side projects

I'm using finals stress as a reason to start two things instead of finishing them (this is bad time management, do not copy):

  • PromptShield - a CLI scanner that takes prompts headed to an LLM and flags injection patterns. It's mostly heuristics right now (instruction overrides, role-confusion, encoded payloads) but I want to add a small classifier trained on the public PromptInjection benchmarks. Why I care: every company I've talked to about AI security is still using if "ignore previous" in prompt. We can do better.
  • HoneyJar - a $5 honeypot. Drop it on a VPS, point ports 22/23/445 at it, get a Discord webhook every time something interesting hits. Built because tuning SIEM rules at Mindboard taught me that the cleanest detection signal is "stuff nobody should be touching."

CTF post-mortem (coming next week)

I owe my HackFax × PatriotHacks team a writeup of what worked and what didn't from this year's Web Attack track. Short version: more testers, fewer cute tricks, and please stop hiding flags in things that require sub-pixel mouse precision. Longer version next post.
